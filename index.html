How I Think About Product Leadership

Simple things should be simple, and complex things should be possible.

Good product design doesn't mean hiding complexity — it means managing it so it doesn't block what users need to do. The baseline experience should be effortless. The edge cases should still work.

Clarity creates momentum.

When a team is uncertain about priorities, they slow down, duplicate work, or hedge their bets. Clear direction — even imperfect direction — allows people to move. I focus on reducing ambiguity before it compounds.

Alignment is part of the product.

Cross-functional trust isn't overhead — it's load-bearing. Products built without alignment show the cracks: mismatched assumptions, conflicting data models, user experiences that feel stitched together. I treat alignment as a core deliverable.

Systems outlast features.

Any individual feature has a short shelf life. The architecture underneath — the data models, the integration patterns, the shared workflows — can last for years. I invest in the durable layer so the product can adapt without rebuilding from scratch.
